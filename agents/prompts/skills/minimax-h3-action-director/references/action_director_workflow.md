# Action Director 5-Step Workflow

## Step 1: Action Style Positioning

### 1.1 Martial Art Selection
Choose from 7 core types with distinct visual signatures:

**Hong Kong Kung Fu (HK)**
- **Pacing:** Rapid, 0.3-0.8s per shot
- **Camera:** Quick cuts, close-ups on contact, follow-cam on combos
- **Framing:** Mix of wide (position), medium (exchange), extreme close (impact)
- **Lighting:** Bright, high-contrast, clinically clear
- **Emotion:** Explosive, virtuosic, entertaining
- **Key techniques:** Shuai Jiao footwork, hand-speed emphasis, environmental use

**Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu (BJJ)**
- **Pacing:** Slow, 2-4s per position transition
- **Camera:** Locked wide or tight close-up, very little camera motion
- **Framing:** Mostly extreme close (faces, limbs, grips), rare wide
- **Lighting:** Intimate, personal, high depth-of-field
- **Emotion:** Methodical, intimate, intellectual
- **Key techniques:** Leverage emphasis, grip strength, positional control, breathing visibility

**Boxing**
- **Pacing:** Medium-fast, 0.8-1.5s per exchange
- **Camera:** Profile angles, head-height camera, hand-speed tracking
- **Framing:** Medium shots showing stance and guard, upper-body focus
- **Lighting:** Ring lights, dramatic backlighting, clear side-lighting
- **Emotion:** Explosive exchange, defensive chess, sudden knockout
- **Key techniques:** Footwork, guard integrity, head movement, torso targeting

**Muay Thai**
- **Pacing:** Medium, 1.0-2.0s per technique
- **Camera:** Clinch-close framing, rotational tracking, leg emphasis
- **Framing:** Wide (clinch positioning), medium (strike selection)
- **Lighting:** Ring/arena, high-key, saturated colors
- **Emotion:** Powerful, rotational, muscular exertion visible
- **Key techniques:** Clinch work, eight-point strikes, knee/elbow emphasis

**Sword Fighting**
- **Pacing:** Medium-slow, 1.5-3.0s per engagement
- **Camera:** Line-based geometry, steel/light reflection emphasis
- **Framing:** Wide (sword extension lines), medium (torso/blade relationship)
- **Lighting:** Reflection/glint work critical, high-key steel emphasis
- **Emotion:** Geometric, precise, extension emphasis
- **Key techniques:** Point clarity, footwork spacing, blade interaction

**Kickboxing**
- **Pacing:** Medium-fast, 0.9-1.8s per exchange
- **Camera:** Hybrid angle (head + legs), rotation tracking
- **Framing:** Full-body emphasis to show leg extension
- **Lighting:** Ring/gym, balanced, clear shadow separation
- **Emotion:** Explosive, hybrid rhythm, power visible
- **Key techniques:** Leg-heavy, stance rotation, balance emphasis

**Professional Wrestling**
- **Pacing:** Slow-medium, dramatic holds before impact
- **Camera:** Wide positioning, theatrical framing, partner coordination
- **Framing:** Full-body to show carry/positioning, crowd-facing
- **Lighting:** Theatrical, high-contrast, exaggerated shadows
- **Emotion:** Dramatic, coordinated, impact exaggeration
- **Key techniques:** Carry dynamics, height differential, staged impact

## Step 2: Scene Need Gathering

### 2.1 Core Scene Parameters

**Duration:** 5-15 seconds (MiniMax H3 standard)
- 5-7s: Very tight, single exchange or quick sequence
- 8-10s: Standard, one full engagement with setup/impact/recovery
- 11-15s: Extended, multiple exchanges or complex choreography

**Character Count:**
- One-on-one: 2 fighters
- Multi-opponent: 1 hero vs 3-5 enemies (or group brawl)
- Pursuit: 2+ moving through space
- Showcase: Solo performer demonstration

**Environment:**
- Warehouse/industrial: Clean, high-contrast, minimal distracting detail
- Rooftop/urban: Height variation, sky background, wind consideration
- Rain/weather: Atmospheric, running/splashing, water reflection
- Corridor/staircase: Architectural framing, vertical or horizontal depth
- Forest/nature: Textural, green palette, tree line background
- Arena/ring: Theatrical lighting, crowd sound layer
- Confined (car, elevator, bathroom): Extreme close-up framing, tight angles

**Conflict Stage:**
- **Approach:** Characters entering frame, sizing up, breathing before contact
- **Exchange:** Active striking/grappling, multiple techniques, back-and-forth
- **Impact:** Maximum force moment, breakthrough/counter/power strike
- **Resolution:** Recovery, reset, exhaustion visible, or knockout

---

### 2.2 Scene Template Mapping

| Template | Duration | Character Setup | Visual Arc | Audio Strategy |
|----------|----------|-----------------|-----------|-----------------|
| One-on-One Duel | 8-10s | 2 equal fighters | Setup → exchange → impact → reset | Impact + breathing sync |
| Multi-Opponent | 10-12s | 1 hero vs 3-5 | Approach → rapid sequence → crowd-based tempo | Impacts layered, crowd reaction |
| Pursuit/Chase | 10-15s | 2 in movement | Anticipation → chase physics → catch/impact | Footsteps, direction audio, impact |
| Defense/Counter | 8-10s | 2 focused on defense | Setup → attack → defense → counter → payoff | Attack sound → counter emphasis |
| Staircase/Corridor | 10-12s | 2+ in architecture | Height variation → movement through space → impact | Reverb from space, footsteps clarity |
| Environmental Use | 10-12s | 2 using environment | Setup → prop acquisition → striking with prop | Object impact variety |
| Weapon Duel | 10-15s | 2 with weapons | Distance-based geometry → engagement → impact → recovery | Blade/hilt contact clarity |
| Desperation Final | 8-10s | 2 exhausted | Setup (breathing heavy) → final exchange → knockout | Labored breathing, final impact emphasis |

## Step 3: Shot Design Architecture

### 3.1 Establishing Shots (1.5-2.5s)
**Purpose:** Set spatial context, introduce both fighters, establish distance and environment

**Camera Strategy:**
- Wide, static or very slow push-in
- Both fighters in frame
- Depth-of-field set to show environment context but keep fighters sharp
- Lighting establishes the environment's color and mood

**Cinematography Apply:**
- Director mood: Coppola = very slow push (3+ seconds for even 2m), Bigelow = static handheld wobble
- Martial art pacing: HK needs quick transition (1.5s max), BJJ needs longer (2-3s for position reading)

**Example prompt fragment:**
```
Wide establishing shot of two martial artists facing off in warehouse. 
Camera: static on tripod, positioned 12 meters back, showing both fighters 
at thirds of frame. Lighting: 60-degree side light from high window, 
creating shadow lines without harsh contrast. Depth of field: f/4.5, 
keeping both fighters in focus while background (shipping containers) 
is out of focus. Breathing in sync, eyes locked, ready stance. No motion. 
Duration: 2.0 seconds.
```

---

### 3.2 Action/Exchange Shots (0.6-1.5s)
**Purpose:** Show technique execution, speed, impact, defensive response

**Camera Strategy:**
- Medium to medium-close
- Profile or 45-degree angle to show clear strike path
- Quick cuts (multiple shots in rapid sequence) or single locked shot
- Facial expression clarity prioritized

**Cinematography Apply:**
- Director mood: Bigelow = handheld tracking at 50% of strike speed, Coppola = mostly static
- Martial art pacing: HK = rapid cuts (0.3-0.8s per shot), BJJ = single long take (2-3s)
- High-speed rule: Apply motion blur directionally (only in strike path), not across whole frame

**Example prompt fragment:**
```
Medium close shot of fighter A delivering rapid 3-strike combo 
(punch-palm-kick). Camera: static, positioned at 45-degree side angle, 
5 meters from fighters. Fighter B's defense shown in profile—block, 
parry, step back. Facial expressions: A concentrated, B focused on defense. 
Clarity strategy: 40% motion blur on striking limbs only; core body 70% 
clarity. Impact sounds clear at each strike connection. Duration: 0.8 seconds.
```

---

### 3.3 Impact/Climax Shots (0.2-0.5s)
**Purpose:** Register maximum force, break contact, show reaction

**Camera Strategy:**
- Extreme close-up (face, limb connection, body compression)
- Static hold (0.2-0.3s minimum)
- High-contrast lighting to show force transmission
- Facial expression during impact critical

**Cinematography Apply:**
- High-speed rule: Static hold frames are maximum clarity. Hold 0.3s minimum at impact.
- Director mood: Coppola = soft focus even on impact (dreamlike quality), Bigelow = sharp close-up clarity
- Audio: Impact sound at maximum volume (auditory emphasis on visual impact)

**Example prompt fragment:**
```
Impact hold: Camera returns to static close-up on contact point. 
Fighter A's punch meets Fighter B's block. Extreme force visible: 
B's arm compresses, shoulders rise, feet brace into concrete, face 
shows pain/effort. Eyes wince. Hold this frame static for 0.3 seconds. 
Audio: Single clean punch-block impact sound at 2.5kHz with subsonic bass.
```

---

### 3.4 Recovery/Transition Shots (1.0-2.0s)
**Purpose:** Reset for next exchange, show exhaustion, build tension

**Camera Strategy:**
- Medium shot showing both fighters or single-fighter close-up
- Camera motion minimal (static or very slow)
- Breathing emphasis (visible chest heave)
- Eye contact or evasion showing psychological state

**Cinematography Apply:**
- Director mood: Coppola = long holds with breathing rhythm, Bigelow = quick reset with tension audio
- Pacing: BJJ stays longest (3+ seconds for position analysis), HK shortest (0.5-1.0s)
- Audio: Breathing sync, ambient warehouse tone, psychological silence

**Example prompt fragment:**
```
Recovery shot: Camera holds static on Fighter A's face and upper body. 
A recovers from impact, resets stance, breathing visible chest heave. 
Eyes track to B. Both fighters reset to ready position. Warehouse ambient 
only, breathing audible. Small footsteps as they circle. Duration: 1.5 seconds.
```

## Step 4: Cinematography & Pacing Application

### 4.1 High-Speed Action Compensation Strategies

**Strategy 1: Slow Push-In (Kurosawa Rule)**
When action is running at maximum speed (strikes, kicks, rapid exchanges):
- Camera slowly pushes in 1-3 meters over 3-6 seconds
- Push speed must be 40-60% of action speed (creates relative clarity)
- Background progressively blurs (provides visual "rest")
- Psychological effect: "Camera getting closer = better detail"

**Strategy 2: Static Hold at Impact (Lee Rule)**
Maximum force moments need maximum clarity registration:
- Hold camera completely static for 0.2-0.3 seconds at moment of contact
- No panning, no zooming, no drift
- Let audience process the impact
- Psychological effect: "Static frame = this moment is important"

**Strategy 3: Arc Tracking (Chan Rule)**
When following rapid lateral movement:
- Camera arcs at 40-60% of movement speed
- Arc radius: 6-10 meters (semicircle path)
- Fighter passes through the arc rather than being directly chased
- Psychological effect: "Relative motion reduced = perceived clarity"

**Strategy 4: Handheld Tension (Bigelow Rule)**
For documentary-verite styles:
- Micro-wobble camera (±1-2 pixels, 2-4 Hz frequency)
- Never smooth tracking; occasional micro-refocus
- Limited but decisive pans and tilts
- Psychological effect: "Handheld = urgent, real-time"

**Strategy 5: Multiple Rapid Cuts (Editing Rule)**
Quick cuts can hide blur at maximum speed:
- Cut to new angle right as blur reaches maximum
- New shot starts with clearer angle or reset action
- 3-5 cuts per 3 seconds maximum (avoids disorientation)
- Psychological effect: "Cut = new information = feels faster"

---

### 4.2 Director-Specific Pacing

**Sofia Coppola:** 
- Hold times: 2.5s minimum for establishing, 1.5s for recovery
- Camera moves: Only slow push-in or ultra-slow arc, never handheld
- Action pacing: Slow down naturally—don't accelerate exchanges
- Impact holds: 0.4-0.5s (longer than standard) for contemplation

**Kathryn Bigelow:**
- Hold times: 0.5-1.0s only (keep tension high)
- Camera moves: Handheld with micro-wobble, frequent reframing
- Action pacing: Rapid cuts, tight editing, never long holds
- Impact holds: 0.15-0.2s (quick confirmation, move on)

**Park Chan-wook:**
- Hold times: 2.0-3.0s for geometric composition shots
- Camera moves: Slow lateral tracking, often off-angle
- Action pacing: Build tension through stillness, then explosive
- Impact holds: 0.3-0.4s with geometric frame emphasis

**Denis Villeneuve:**
- Hold times: 2.5s for landscape/context, 1.0s for action
- Camera moves: Slow push-in, very wide shots, landscape emphasis
- Action pacing: Patient setup, fast exchange, slow recovery
- Impact holds: 0.25-0.3s with wide frame context

## Step 5: Timing Chart & Prompt Generation

### 5.1 Build 0.1-Second Timing Chart

Create a markdown table with these columns:

| Time (0.1s) | Camera State | Fighter A Action | Fighter B Action | Audio Events |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0-1.5s | Static wide, 12m back, no motion | Ready stance, breathing sync | Ready stance, breathing sync | Warehouse ambient, distant drip |
| 1.5-2.0s | Slow push-in toward fighters | Slight circle, eyes tracking | Mirror stance, watching | Footsteps, breathing quickens |
| 2.0-2.8s | Hold close-up on A | 3-strike combo: punch-palm-kick | Defensive blocks each | Impacts: 3x clear contact sounds |
| 2.8-3.5s | Slow arc tracking left | Reset, distance control | Counter kick launch | Kick whistle, B exhales |
| 3.5-3.8s | Impact hold frame static | Block position, arm compression | Kick connects, force visible | Impact thud, pain grunt |
| 3.8-4.5s | Static on A's face | Recovery, breathing deep, eyes reset | Stance reset, circling | Labored breathing, ambience |

**Budgeting Rules:**
- Establishing shots: 1.5-2.5s minimum (let composition breathe)
- Held beats: 2.5s minimum if no camera motion
- Slow camera moves: 3-6s typical for 2-3 meter movement
- Fast camera move: 0.3-1.0s maximum
- Strike exchange: 0.6-1.2s per camera angle
- Impact: 0.2-0.5s hold (minimum 0.2s to register)
- Recovery: 1.0-2.0s to show exhaustion/reset

**Validation:**
- Sum all time blocks = target duration ✓
- High-priority beat (climax) not in final 10% ✓
- Timing matches director pacing expectations ✓

---

### 5.2 Convert Chart to Prompt Syntax

Use this structure (from VIDEO_PROMPT_WRITING_GUIDE_base_en.md):

```
Task type: T2VA (or I2VA/FL2VA/L2VA as applicable)
Duration: [target] seconds

[Shot 1] At 0.0s, [camera state and composition]
[narrative description of action and movement]
[audio layer description]

[Shot 2] At [time], [camera state]
[narrative description]
[audio layer]

... continue for all shot blocks ...

integrated_multimodal_description: [one paragraph synthesizing visual mood, color palette, director style, action clarity strategy]

overall_soundscape: [describe all audio layers: ambient, impacts, breathing, reverb, dynamics]

non_diegetic_music: [describe music treatment: instrumental, silence, sustained notes, rhythm]
```

**Key rules:**
- One shot block `[Shot N]` only at true cuts (major camera angle or distance change)
- Every action as observable behavior, never emotion
- Every timestamp to 0.1s precision
- Camera motion described as natural prose, not technical specs
- Static shots must forbid drift: "Camera remains completely static; no pan, tilt, or drift"

## Master Checklist Before Delivery

- ✓ Task type (T2VA/I2VA/etc.) and duration stated correctly
- ✓ Static shots explicitly forbid drift (don't just say "static")
- ✓ All actions are observable behavior (not emotion/motivation)
- ✓ Timing chart sums exactly to target duration
- ✓ Highest-priority beat positioned before final 10% of duration
- ✓ Director style evident in pacing, framing, and color choices
- ✓ Martial art choreography authentic to the style selected
- ✓ High-speed cinematography rules applied (slow push-in / impact holds / arc tracking as needed)
- ✓ All cuts and transitions timed to 0.1s precision
- ✓ Audio design supports visual narrative (impacts clear, breathing synchronized)
- ✓ Prompt is under 2000 tokens (H3 efficiency requirement)
